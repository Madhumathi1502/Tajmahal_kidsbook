### 2. Archaeological Survey of India (ASI)
- **URL:** https://asi.nic.in/taj-mahal/
- **Authority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Used for:** Official government authority on the monument; confirmation of construction dates, materials, workforce, artisan regions, marble source (Makrana), and the complex's components
- **Key facts sourced:**
  - Construction began: 1632
  - Mausoleum completed: 1648
  - Complex completed: 1653
  - White marble from Makrana, Rajasthan
  - Semiprecious stones sourced from multiple regions
  - ASI is the custodian body responsible for conservation

- **URL:** https://tajmahal.gov.in/
- **Authority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### 7. Ebba Koch — Scholarly Research on the Taj Mahal
- **Authority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Ebba Koch is a leading academic authority on Mughal architecture and the Taj Mahal)
- **Accessed via:** Scholarly summaries citing Koch's research (ResearchGate, SciSpace, scribd.com academic papers)
- **Used for:** Riverfront garden design concept, placement of mausoleum at northern end not the centre, Mughal charbagh traditions, garden landscape design analysis
- **Key scholarly contributions:**
  - The Taj Mahal uses a "riverfront garden plan" — placing the mausoleum at the north of the garden, not the centre
  - This is a departure from traditional charbagh models
  - The Yamuna River functions as a "cross-axis" of the design
  - Scholarly characterisation of the complex as a "funerary and utilitarian construct"

## NOTES ON DISPUTED OR CAUTIOUS CLAIMS

The following claims appear in many popular sources but are either disputed, unsupported, or require cautious handling. They are flagged in the fact sheet and must be handled carefully in the manuscript.

1. **The name of the principal architect** — Multiple names are cited; none is definitively established by the strongest scholarly authorities. The book will not name a specific architect as certain.

2. **The exact number of workers** — "20,000" is an estimate, not a census figure. Always presented as "approximately."

3. **The original planting of the garden** — The garden has been altered since the 17th century. Specific original planting lists are not used.

4. **The copper-pot fountain mechanism** — Interesting engineering detail found in secondary sources. Will be described as "according to historical accounts" if included.

5. **The minarets' purpose** — Not definitively documented. Visual description only.

6. **The charbagh as a paradise symbol** — Described as "associated with garden traditions connected to ideas of paradise" using scholarly language, not as a proven symbolic intent.
